# Aaron Mendoza

## Consciousness & Freedom
I used to understand consciousness as a guaranteed part of my being. I am who I am because I am conscious is what I told myself. I never really saw it as a thing that I could be missing or “without”, I just always thought I was conscious.

I understand freedom as the ability to do what you want whenever you want without any immediate consequences from doing the action.

## Continuity of Identity
I believe that I hold the same identity throughout life but not in another. While I think that identity can change over time it is still connected to the same core beliefs that anchor us. I kind of think of identity as a big tree and at the base of the three is the trunk which holds our closest thoughts and beliefs. Slowly we branch out from the core value but in the end it is all still connected to that central “trunk”. It does not matter that small parts of your identity change as long as you are part of that larger central structure. However, this does not carry onto a new life. The tree is only permanent to one life.

## Repetitive Behavior That Changed Me
Repetitive behavior in the gym trained me physically to become stronger but to also value my time and self more. It changed my attitude for the better and made me feel more “in the moment”. The gym was where I went where I could self reflect on my day or actions. It reprogrammed me to become a more persistent person.

## Detachment vs. Discipline
To me, the discipline model works better for explaining my experiences. I feel that most of my desired and undesired outcomes have come from my habitual actions in my life. I feel that what I do on the day to day is what explains what happens to me. By being detached “nothing” happens, I need to “do something” in order to accomplish something.

## Scarry on Consciousness
Scarry’s main account of consciousness is projecting consciousness onto objects. In Westworld, Ford is directly attempting to apply a living consciousness to a machine. Additionally, she explores how you can only “feel” feelings, there is not one true feeling of what an individual feeling was like.
