Journal

  1. Consciousness & Freedom

    I understand consciousness to be a developed part of our brain that either:

    • sits back while neurons are fired and creates an illusion that we have autonomy over our thoughts and what is happening
    • is an individual part of ourselves that desires social interaction to be fully formed and be an individual entity within the brain

    I think freedom is not being under control of a separate entity. It means I have autonomy over decisions, feelings, reactions and nothing else determines that for me.

  2. Continuity of Identity

    I believe that I change throughout the course of my life but it is still me. My younger self and current self share similar qualities and traits no matter how much has changed in between, meaning I still have a grounded identity somewhere, even if it isn’t entirely defined yet. I do think after reincarnation or a memory wipe, I would lose defining parts of me which would, in turn, affect my identity and bring up the question if it was me at all.

  3. Repetitive Behavior That Changed Me

    Any new skill I’ve acquired throughout my life has required some level of training. In school, sports, at home, etc… And while the physical practice is what gets you the results, I find that it taught me more about appreciating the practice itself over the results. You learn a specific kind of discipline and commitment when you are dedicated to something, and that, I believe, has changed me. I have matured from experiences where the practice was more valuable than the payout. I have understood my personal values better from a deep commitment to trying something new.

  4. Detachment vs. Discipline

    I would lean towards discipline in my experience because I am made of my experiences in life and what I did with them. I believe there have been formative, repeated, experiences that would be defining when talking about my “consciousness.” To some degree, things happen and cannot be explained, but I think repetition and imitation is a pinnacle to achieving successful introspection, or, consciousness.

  5. Scarry on Consciousness

    She talks about projection of human sentience onto external objects and how we then turn indifferent objects into something that owes us (whether it be safety, care, convenience). She also argues that phenomenality cannot be reduced to physical qualities.
